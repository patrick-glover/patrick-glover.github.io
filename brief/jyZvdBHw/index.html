1. Paternal Valproate Exposure and Offspring Neurodevelopmental Outcomes

Paternal valproate exposure is associated with adverse neurodevelopmental outcomes in offspring, extending teratogenicity concerns beyond the well-established maternal exposure risks. 4. Dendritic translation and neuroproteasome-mediated degradation of endogenous tau revealed by STARFISH

STARFISH, a new method for visualizing endogenous mRNA translation in situ, reveals that tau is translated exclusively in neuronal dendrites — not the soma — and is rapidly turned over by local neuroproteasomes. When this dendritic proteasomal degradation fails, tau accumulates and forms aggregates, providing a mechanistic framework for early tauopathy. This localizes tau homeostasis to a previously underappreciated subcellular compartment and implicates dendritic proteostasis as a therapeutic target in Alzheimer's disease and related tauopathies.

Nature Neuroscience · Aug 13

5. Cross-region neuron co-firing mediated by ripple oscillations supports distributed working memory representations

Human intracranial recordings demonstrate that neurons across bilateral corticolimbic sites co-fire in a working memory-dependent manner, with stimulus-selective firing reinstated when both regions transiently oscillate at ~90 Hz (ripple frequency). This provides direct mechanistic evidence that high-frequency ripple coupling orchestrates long-distance neural integration underlying working memory, extending findings from rodent spatial memory to human cognitive function. The corticolimbic network implicated is highly relevant to temporal lobe epilepsy, where ripple-generating circuits are frequently disrupted or resected.

6. The Prognostic Value of Paramagnetic Rim Lesions in People With Multiple Sclerosis

Paramagnetic rim lesions (PRLs) on susceptibility-weighted MRI — reflecting chronic active demyelination driven by iron-laden microglia at lesion borders — carry significant prognostic value for future disability progression in multiple sclerosis. Across sources, PRL burden at baseline predicts progression independent of relapse activity, lending support to their utility as a biomarker for smoldering neuroinflammation. This has direct implications for treatment stratification and trial design, particularly as therapeutics targeting chronic active lesions (e.g., BTK inhibitors) enter late-stage development.
