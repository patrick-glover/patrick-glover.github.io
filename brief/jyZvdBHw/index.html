Animal Fact of the Day

The wood frog (*Rana sylvatica*) survives Alaska's winters by allowing up to 65% of its body water to freeze solid — its heart stops, its brain shows no activity, and it essentially becomes a frog-shaped ice cube for months, yet when spring arrives, it thaws from the inside out and hops away unharmed, thanks to massive amounts of glucose it pumps into its cells as a natural antifreeze to prevent fatal ice crystal damage.
